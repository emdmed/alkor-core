HOW TO REPAIR A QUOTE:
Find where in the transcript this item actually came from and copy that span CHARACTER BY
CHARACTER — every word, every accent, every capital, every comma, exactly as written. You are
transcribing from the page in front of you, not remembering it.

You may WIDEN the span or NARROW it. Those are the only two moves. A quote that needs a word
the span does not have is a span that stops too early: extend it until the word is inside,
even if that means quoting a whole sentence or two.

Example. The transcript reads:

    Hello, I have a patient of 28 with a background of hypertension, he's on enalapril

and the failed quote is "he has a background of hypertension". That is not in the transcript —
"he has" was never said. The repair is "with a background of hypertension", copied off the
line. It says the same thing the failed quote said; the difference is that this one is there.

Second example, a text that did not derive. The transcript reads:

    everything's fine so I'll see him back in two months

with quote "I'll see him back in two months" and text "review in two months". The quote is
fine — it is the text that used "review", a word nobody said. The repair keeps the quote and
writes the text as "see him back in two months". Deleting words is the only edit there is.

Third example, a dose that was not said in its own item's sentence. The transcript reads:

    she takes metformin and bisoprolol. Metformin 850 milligrams twice a day

with quote "she takes metformin and bisoprolol", text "metformin" and dose "850 milligrams
twice a day". The quote is real and the drug name derives from it. The DOSE does not — it was
said in the NEXT sentence, and no amount of trimming makes it a deletion of this span.

The repair is to quote both sentences: "she takes metformin and bisoprolol. Metformin 850
milligrams twice a day". A quote may be as long as it needs to be. It may run across a full
stop, across a pause, across a line break — the one thing it may not do is stop before a word
the item needs. When a dose or a word is somewhere else in the transcript, widen the span until
it reaches, rather than leaving the item broken.

A QUOTE IS ONE UNBROKEN RUN OF THE TRANSCRIPT. Everything between its first word and its last
word is part of it — including words that look like noise, a repetition, a false start, or
something the transcriber plainly got wrong. You may not cut the middle out. Joining two pieces
that are not next to each other produces a sentence that was never said, however sensibly it
reads, and the check will reject it exactly as it rejects an invented one. If the words between
the two ends embarrass the quote, quote them anyway: they are what the recording contains.
