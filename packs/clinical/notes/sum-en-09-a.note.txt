MEDICAL ADMISSIONS UNIT - CLERKING

Admitted overnight from the emergency department with four days of productive
cough, fever and increasing breathlessness on minimal exertion. She is 76 and
lives alone in a first-floor flat with no lift, managing her own shopping and
cooking until this week.

History of chronic obstructive pulmonary disease, diagnosed eleven years ago
and followed in the respiratory clinic since. Two admissions with exacerbations
in the last three years, neither requiring ventilatory support. Also has
osteoporosis, diagnosed after a wrist fracture in 2019, and long-standing
gastro-oesophageal reflux.

Ex-smoker, forty pack-years, stopped at the time of her diagnosis. No alcohol.
No occupational dust exposure that she can recall.

Regular medication on admission, confirmed against the repeat prescription her
daughter brought in: tiotropium 18 micrograms once daily by inhaler,
beclometasone with formoterol two puffs twice daily, salbutamol as required,
alendronic acid 70 mg once weekly on a Sunday, and omeprazole 20 mg each
morning. She takes no anticoagulant and no diuretic.

Allergies: penicillin, described as a rash in childhood, never investigated.

On examination she is unwell but not peri-arrest. Widespread wheeze with
scattered coarse crackles at the right base. No peripheral oedema. Abdomen soft.
Calves soft and non-tender.

Chest radiograph shows right basal consolidation on a background of
hyperinflated lung fields. Bloods show a raised white cell count and a C-reactive
protein of 214. Renal function is normal. Venous gas shows a mild respiratory
acidosis with a bicarbonate suggesting chronic retention.

Impression: community-acquired pneumonia on a background of chronic obstructive
pulmonary disease, with an infective exacerbation.

Plan: admit to the medical ward. Given the penicillin allergy, start
clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily intravenously and doxycycline 100 mg once
daily. Controlled oxygen to a target saturation of 88 to 92 per cent. Regular
nebulised salbutamol and ipratropium. Prednisolone 30 mg once daily for five
days. Continue her inhalers when she is able to use them. Fluids, and repeat
bloods in the morning. Physiotherapy referral made overnight. Resuscitation
status discussed with her and with her daughter by telephone.
