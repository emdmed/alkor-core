WARD ROUND - DAY 3

A better night. She slept in four-hour stretches and coughed less. Her oxygen
requirement has come down and she is now on one litre per minute by nasal
cannula, saturating comfortably within her target range.

She has been afebrile for over twenty-four hours. On that basis the intravenous
clarithromycin is switched to oral clarithromycin 500 mg twice daily today, as
planned yesterday. Doxycycline continues. This is day three of the five-day
prednisolone course.

Examination: air entry improved throughout, wheeze much reduced, occasional
crackles at the right base. Chest is clearer than yesterday. No oedema, calves
soft.

Bloods: C-reactive protein 94 and falling, white cell count now normal. Renal
function normal. The sputum culture has grown no significant organism, which is
reported and does not change the plan.

The physiotherapist reports good sputum clearance and she is now walking to the
bathroom with a frame and one person. The occupational therapist has seen her
and has raised the question of a stair assessment before discharge, since the
flat has a flight of stairs and no lift. That assessment has not yet been done.

Her daughter asked about the vaccinations she is due. She has had the current
influenza vaccine but has never had a pneumococcal vaccination; this is noted so
that it can be arranged with the general practitioner after discharge rather
than during the acute illness.

Plan: continue oral antibiotics, wean the oxygen as tolerated, restart the usual
inhalers once nebulisers are reduced to as-required. Aim for discharge early
next week if the stair assessment is satisfactory.

She asked this morning about how long the antibiotics would go on for and about
whether she would be able to manage the stairs at home. Both were answered on the
round and her daughter was telephoned afterwards with the same information, so
that the two accounts agree. She was reassured and asked no further questions.
Pressure areas remain intact and the fluid chart is adequate.
